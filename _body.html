Cartesian sticker cutter

A desktop machine that draws a sticker in pen, swaps to a knife and cuts it out — the first build in BU’s Electromechanical Design course, taken from a foam-board plotter to an aluminum gantry.

  • 2.5 degrees of freedom: belt-driven X and Y axes on 2020 aluminum extrusion with NEMA 17 steppers, plus a double rack-and-pinion lift that raises and lowers the tool.
  • One end effector holds either a pen or a 360° swivel knife, so the same machine draws the design and then cuts around it.
  • 3D-printed motor and idler mounts modeled in Onshape; the machine runs G-code through Repetier-Host.
  • Six working sticker designs, in a machine small enough to carry in a large bag — one of the brief’s constraints.

Drawing, then cutting

How it works

  • An image-to-G-code script (Python and OpenCV, written by a teammate) turns any picture into two passes: a hatched pen drawing, then a cut around the outline at a set offset.
  • The bed carries the sticker sheet along one axis while the gantry carries the tool along the other; set screws clamp whichever pen you want to draw with.
  • Constraints from the brief: at least 2.5 degrees of freedom, built on 2020 extrusion, small enough to fit in a large bag.

Build

Where it started: a foam-board plotter

Before any aluminum, the team built a one-axis stage — a stepper turning an 8 mm lead screw that drives a carriage along a foam-board base — then stacked two of them into an X–Y plotter run from Arduino and fed G-code made in F-Engrave. The foam flexed, and the motors had enough torque to knock the stages out of line, so the team slowed them until drawings held and moved to extrusion for the cutter.

Team project, four people. Several photos and both drawing videos come from teammate Samuel Smith’s ME 360 course portfolio.

Container-door linkage

A four-bar linkage that swings a full-height door on a 20 ft ISO container from closed to stowed flat on the roof, without the door ever passing through the box. The linkage mounts on the container’s side wall.

  • Found the two fixed pivots by graphical synthesis: drew the door at three positions and intersected the perpendicular bisectors of each pin’s path. Both pivots land on the side wall.
  • The 8 ft door is the coupler, with a 5.86 ft crank, an 8.58 ft rocker and 7.09 ft between pivots — a Grashof crank-rocker, so a motor on the crank pivot could drive it. The crank turns 243° from closed to stowed.
  • Modeled and animated in Onshape: container, door, links and pins as separate parts, assembled with revolute mates.

3D model

Clearance

  • With the door closed, the two link lines cross a little below its top edge, so the top of the door swings inward for the first part of the stroke. A small gap at the container’s front fixes it: anything over 0.13 ft clears the corner, and 0.2 ft leaves margin.
  • Past fully open, the same linkage would carry the door down into the roof, so the crank needs a hard stop at 243° — mate limits in the model.
